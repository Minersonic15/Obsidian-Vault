# Structure
Crystalline solids are mostly ionic or covalent bonds. [[9.28.26 Chemical Bonding]]
- Crystalline solids are where the constituents are arranged in a highly ordered lattice structure in all directions
- Metals can also exist as crystalline solids

Ionic bonding resulted from electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged [[9.16.26 Ions]]
- but metals are neutral atoms

> [!NOTE] THINGY
> When arranged in a lattice, the outer valence electrons are strongly attracted to neighbouring atoms,
> moving through the empty spaces in lattice away from original atom

- These electrons are shared among all metal atoms in a lattice, as valence electrons are not bound to any one specific atom as they are **DELOCALISED ELECTRONS** and are free to move throughout lattice
- As valence electrons are delocalised, the remaining atoms in lattice become positively charged
- ts cations repel eachother because have same charge, and keep the lattice in place
	- strong electrostatic forces

Metallic bonding is unique from ionic/covalent as the bonding is between cations within lattice structure and delocal electrons, not atoms/elecs
-despite bonding metals has cations, they are neutral because all valence remain on part of struct

# Physical Properties and application of mets
Properties of metals include:
- Lustre (shiny)
- Sonority (sound when struck)
- Malleability (reshapability)
- Ductility (make wire)
- Electrical conductivity
- Thermal Conductivity

**Lustre is reflected light:**
- When visible light hits surface of metal, electrons absorb some energy and vibrate
- ts generates more light wave which radiates from surface, ts is what makes it reflective and shiny
**Sonority:**
- When a metal surface is struck, the free electrons can move easily, propagating incoming sound easily through material.
- lower density, more space between cations
	- more sonority
**Malleability + Ductility**
- electrostatic attraction happens in all directions
- when stress is applied, layers in lattice shift in response
- as shift, the cations remain surrounded by valence, meaning metallic bond is unaffected,
**Electrical Conductivity**
- ability of charged particles to move through region of space
- Delocalised valence electrons can move throughout the lattice
- when potential energy difference is applied, the delocalised electrons are repelled by the negative terminal and attracted to the positive
- As valence electrons num increases, num of delocalised electrons moving increases.
	- ts increases electrical conductivity
- ionic compounds do not conduct electricity, there are no charged particles moving
**Thermal Conductivity**
- measure of materials ability to transfer heat as a result of temperature difference.

| Temperature                                              | Kinetic energy                              | Heat/thermal energy                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Measure of average kinetic energy of particles in system | The energy a particle has due to its motion | transfer of kinetic energy from one region to another as result of diff in temperature |

- Result of free electrons in lattice.
- when region increases temperature, kinetic energy of delocalised elecs increases
- Ts delocalised electrons travel towards lower temp region of lattice
# Strength of the metallic bond
The stength of electrostatic force of attraction between delocalised elecs and cations is dependent on same factors as ionic bonding
- These factors are radius of metal ion and ionic charge [[9.17.26 Periodicity]], which determines electon density.
- THe smaller radius of metal ion, stronger metallic bond. shorter distance between positive nucleus of cation and surrounding elecs
- Higher ionic charge, stronger bond
	- Greater charge on metal ion
	- greater num of delocalised valence

- Higher strength results in high melting points for metals
- At room temp, all metals solid but mercury
- Greater strength = harder

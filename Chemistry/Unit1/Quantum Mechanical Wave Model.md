
# Dudes and their stuff
De Broglie:
	- Electrons thought of as wave of energy instead of particle
	- Wavelength: $Lambda = h / mv$
	- Variables:
		- Lambda = wavelength
		- h = planks constant (in [[Chemistry data Booklet]] )
		- m = mass of particle
		- v = velocity
	- Works for all particles with Mass
Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle (1927)
	- it is inherently possible to know both the velocity and location of an electron
	
De Broglie + Heisenberg
	- With respect to nucleus, we cannot know where an electron is
	- Instead, we can know hte probability of finding an electron in a specific region of space
	- Electrons not particles, but waves
	- Electrons exist in orbitals
Bohrs imperfect model
	- Electrons further away from nucleus have more energy [[Atomic Structure]]
# Quantum Numbers
Principal Quantum Number:
	- Whole numbers (n)
	- Relates to distance the electron is from nucleus
	- Tells energy level (n=3, third energy level)
	- Describes suze of orbit
Secondary Quantum Number:
	- Defines shape of orbital
	- Number of values determined by value of "n"
	- Each value of "n"   l = n - 1

| n   | l = n-1 | orbital shape | # of orbital shapes | description of largest orbital |
| --- | ------- | ------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| 1   | 0       | s             | 1                   | Sphere (s)                     |
| 2   | 0, 1    | s, p          | 2                   | Propeller (p)                  |
| 3   | 0, 1, 2 | s, p, d       | 3                   | Dumbells+Donuts (d) ?          |
| 4   | 0,1,2,3 | s,p,d,f       | 4                   | Fun (f) ?                      |
**Orbital shapes not needed to know. just for f**un
 Secondary Quantum Numbers (cont):
	 - Electron Shell (collection of orbitals with same principle quantum number)
	 - Subshell (One or more orbitals with same set of n and l values)
	 - Within a given shell, subshells are in order of increasing energy (4s < 4p < 4d < 4f) - increasing energy

Magnetic Quantum Number (row)
	- m^1 = Orientation in space of the orbital (3D information)
	- Split subshells into individual orbits
	- An orbital can hold two electrons total
		- May hold 0,1,2, but never more

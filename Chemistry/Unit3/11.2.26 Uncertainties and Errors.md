[[9.15.26 Types of Errors, Accuracy and Precision]]
Add and subtract uncertainty from measuring instruments to find precision,
- Less uncertainty, more precise
- Uncertainty is say, smallest unit for example
# Precision
Degree of exactness to which measurement of a quantity can be reproduced
- Depends on finest division of scale on measuring instrument
- Read values from bottom of meniscus of a liquid
- Could also find mass of the liquid instead
	- 1ml=1g
	- D=m/v
# Accuracy
Diff between measured (Experimented) value and accepted value

Remember use [[9.9.26 Units and Conversions]] on ALL answers

# Significant Digits!!!
[[9.10.26 Significant Figures and Scientific Notation]] 
- Non-zero numbers always sig
- Zeros between non-zeros are sig
- Any Zero to the right of a non-zero AND is to the right of decimal point is sig
- Zeros are not sig if they just placeholders, or representing a magnitude [[9.16.26 Order of Magnitude and Rounding]]
- 
	- 4000km = 1SF
	- 4000.0km = 5SF
- SFs gotta be in a row
# Adding / Subtracting Sig Figs
- **WE ALWAYS GIVE 2 ANSWERS, 1 IS ALL NUMS, SECOND IS JUST THE SIGS**
- Final answer must have same num of decimal places as measurement with least precision (Least Decimal Place)
# Multiplying / Dividing SFs
- Final answer must have same number of sig figs as measurement with the least SFs
